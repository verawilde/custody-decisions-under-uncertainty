Decision under uncertainty · family court

Supervise or transfer?

A mother reports that the father has harmed the child. The court usually cannot establish whether it happened. This calculator compares the court's three options by expected harm to the child, and shows that keeping the child with the mother and supervising contact beats transfer to the father at every probability of abuse, as long as supervision lowers the child's exposure and costs less than separation.

All harms are measured in one unit: the harm of separating a child from the primary caregiver. The default values are illustrative, not estimates.

    Independent evidence about the mother
    Only independent evidence counts here. Her abuse report is not such evidence: it is what a protective parent would do if the abuse happened.

    The model

    Expected harm to the child under each option, in units of the harm of separation (σ = 1):

    Supervised contact E[H|S] = b + p·h·e(S) + q·hₘ Unsupervised/shared E[H|U] = p·h·e(U) + q·hₘ Transfer to father E[H|T] = σ + p·h·e(T) + q·hₘ·(1 − Δeₘ)

    With no independent evidence against the mother (q = 0), transfer minus supervision is (σ − b) + p·h·(e(T) − e(S)). That is positive at every p whenever σ > b and e(T) ≥ e(S), so supervision wins without anyone estimating how often mothers' reports are true.

    Companion to Vera Wilde, “Mothers' Abuse Reports Under Uncertainty: Why Custody Courts' Default Response Fails the Child” (preprint, under peer review). The sensitivity analysis code reproduces the paper's tables.
